Before you start

Set the stage

Goal

Shift the prospect’s mindset from viewing Matillion as a “tooling vendor” to an “agentic provider” and a digital data team.

Audience

Above-the-line personas: CDOs, CIOs and data leaders.

Format

Live drawing, with no pre-built slides. Highly Socratic: keep checking for agreement with “Does that sound right?”

The vibe

Conversational and curious. This is the appetizer before any feature demo. Drive engagement, ask questions, and identify your technical champion in the room.

Step 1

The pre-AI enterprise data team (left side)

Draw

  1. Draw a stick figure on the far left and label it “Stakeholders.”
  2. Draw an arrow from Stakeholders to a large circle and label the arrow “Outcomes.”
  3. Label the large circle “Enterprise Data Team.”
  4. Inside the circle, draw three nodes connected in a triangle: People, Tribal Knowledge and Tools.
  5. Write “$8” next to People and “$2” next to Tools.
  6. Below the circle, list the legacy tools (Fivetran, dbt, Informatica, Snowflake, Alteryx, METL) and write “60% Maintenance.”
  7. Optional, to explain the numbers: sketch a bar for “Every $10” split $8 people and $2 tools, and a capacity bar under “60% Maintenance” split into keeping the lights on and new outcomes.

Say

  • “Mr./Mrs. CDO, today you have stakeholders across the business, such as finance and marketing, peppering you with requests. They just want outcomes. And they rely on your Enterprise Data Team to deliver them.”
  • “This team is made up of three things: People, Tribal Knowledge, and Tools.”
  • “Let’s talk about People. It takes about 18 months to fully ramp a data engineer, and the average tenure is roughly 2 years. So you really only get 6 months of true productivity out of them. If you spend $10 on your data team today, about $8 of that goes to the people, right?” (Wait for a nod.)
  • “Those people rely on Tribal Knowledge, stuff living in PDFs, SharePoint, or just in their heads, and they use Tools. You’re probably using things like Fivetran, dbt, Snowflake, or even legacy Matillion ETL.”
  • “The reality? About 60% of this team’s capacity is lost just maintaining existing pipelines, creating a massive Jira backlog.”

What the numbers mean

$8 and $2. This is the cost split of a data team: of every $10 spent, roughly $8 goes to people and $2 to tools. People are the biggest line item, and they carry a hidden cost. It takes about 18 months to ramp a data engineer and average tenure is about 2 years, so you only get around 6 months of full productivity, and their knowledge leaves with them.

60% Maintenance. About 60% of the team’s capacity goes to keeping existing pipelines running, such as fixing breakages and handling source, schema and upgrade changes, rather than building new things. That leaves roughly 40% for new outcomes, which is why requests pile up in the Jira backlog.

Ask: “What share of your team’s week goes to keeping existing pipelines running?”

Both are talk-track figures. Confirm the source before you quote them, and swap in the customer’s own numbers when you have them. The footnote on the board says the figures are illustrative.

Step 2

The divide (center)

Draw

  1. Draw a bold vertical line straight down the middle of the board.
  2. Label the top of the line “AI.”

Say

  • “Unless you’ve been living under a rock, you know AI has arrived. Everything we just drew on the left is the Pre-AI world. Let’s talk about the Post-AI world, which is already a reality for our customers.”

Step 3

The post-AI digital data team (right side)

Draw

  1. Draw a new, equally large circle on the right and label it “Digital Data Team.”
  2. Write “Maia Foundation” at the bottom, replacing Tools.
  3. Write “Maia Teams” on the left, replacing People.
  4. Write “Context Engine” on the right, replacing Tribal Knowledge.
  5. Do not draw people inside this circle.

Say

  • “We have fundamentally rebuilt the data team for this Post-AI era. It maps 1-to-1 with what you already have.”
  • “First, your Tools become Maia Foundation. This is our SaaS platform, Git-backed, highly secure, and built entirely on a YAML-based Data Pipeline Language (DPL). It pushes down into your existing compute like Snowflake.”
  • “Because DPL is human-readable, we were able to layer frontier models on top of it. So your People are replaced inside this circle by Maia Teams, an autonomous, agentic AI data team.”
  • “But Maia Teams needs to know how your business works. That’s where the Context Engine comes in. It replaces human Tribal Knowledge by crawling your Snowflake, Databricks, BigQuery, and even unstructured PDFs to build a semantic knowledge graph. It teaches Maia Teams to speak the language of your business.”

Step 4

Mission Control

Draw

  1. Draw a box sitting on top of the Digital Data Team circle and label it “Mission Control.”
  2. Draw an arrow from the top right, a “Jira / DevOps Backlog,” flowing down into Mission Control.

Say

  • “So, how do you manage this digital team? Through Mission Control.”
  • “We aren’t replacing Jira. Your stakeholders still submit requests there. But instead of those jobs going to a human’s to-do list, they sync directly into Mission Control, which issues the jobs to be done down to Maia Teams.”

Step 5

The elevated human role

Draw

  1. Draw your “People” stick figure outside and to the right of the Digital Data Team circle.
  2. Draw arrows from the people back into Mission Control and the Digital Data Team.
  3. Write three words next to them: Guidance, Permissions, Reviewing.

Say

  • “So, what happens to your expensive, highly skilled data engineers? They get a promotion. They are no longer grinding through point-and-click coding.”
  • “They sit outside the digital team and perform three elevated functions:”
  • Guidance: “Crafting the jobs-to-be-done, talking to stakeholders, and setting architectural standards.”
  • Permissions: “Setting up Git repos, securing data sources, and managing RBAC.”
  • Reviewing: “QA-ing the pipelines and exception handling what Maia Teams produces.”

Objection handling Only if prompted

Amdahl’s Law and copilots

This is step 7 of the walkthrough, so it draws in the bottom corner when you reach it. If the question comes up earlier, press O or use the Copilot objection button to draw it on demand. It clears when you move to the close.

Draw

  1. If they mention Claude Code, CoCo or Genie, draw a small timeline in the bottom corner: Intake, Design, Build, Test, Deploy, Docs, Ops.
  2. Shade in just the “Build” phase for copilots.
  3. Below it, draw a highly compressed version of the whole timeline and write “40x.”
  4. Write the anchor phrase boldly: “Maia is a HARNESS.”

Say

  • If they ask, “My engineers already use Claude Code / Snowflake Cortex”:
  • “Those are great copilots. But think about Amdahl’s Law. A copilot inside a legacy tool only speeds up one or two steps of the pipeline, maybe the coding or the design. It’s a point-in-time task.”
  • “Maia condenses the entire lifecycle end-to-end. We’re talking roughly 40x faster. Point solutions give you a good POC but rack up massive token costs and lack enterprise governance.”
  • “If you take away one thing, it’s this: Maia is a harness. It takes frontier models and wraps them in validated, tested, Git-backed, enterprise-secure delivery of outcomes.”

Wrap it up

The close

Draw

  1. Three options appear at the bottom right: 1-day workshop, 1-week POV, 1-month full program.

Say

  • “Our goal isn’t just to make your team type SQL slightly faster. It’s to clear that Jira backlog and change how outcomes are delivered to your business.”
  • “The next step here is to stop talking and start proving it. Let’s get hands-on. We can set it up in your environment and put it through its paces. Depending on what you need to see, we usually do this via a 1-day workshop, a 1-week POV, or a 1-month full program.”
  • “What timeline makes sense for you to see this live?”
