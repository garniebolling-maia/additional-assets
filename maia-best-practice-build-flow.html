Step 1 of 7

Set up your foundations

Start with the structure a data team needs before the first pipeline: a project connected to your cloud data warehouse, environments that separate development, staging and production, and a Git workflow that suits your team.

Teams of three or more usually work well with feature branches. Smaller teams can use trunk-based development. Project roles control who can build, branch and run.

Maia capabilities

EnvironmentsGit branchesAccess roles

Maia works with GitHub, GitLab, Bitbucket, Azure DevOps and Maia Hosted Git.

Step 2 of 7

Give Maia your standards

Write your naming conventions, data standards and business rules once. Context files apply every time Maia Team responds, and skills add task-specific guidance when Maia Team judges it relevant.

A knowledge graph, built from your warehouse metadata and pipeline history, gives Maia Team the tables, columns, relationships and business meaning to work from.

Maia capabilities

Context filesSkillsKnowledge graph

Write rules as clear directives, such as “Always prefix staging tables with stg_”. Context files live in your project under .matillion/maia/rules/.

Step 3 of 7

Plan the work

State the goal, name the tables and pipelines involved, and point to project files with @. For complex jobs, switch on plan mode and Maia Team presents a step-by-step plan without changing anything, so you can adjust it before it acts.

Assign larger jobs as tasks in Mission Control and track them across projects. Slash commands such as /explain, /document, /fix and /optimize standardize common requests.

Maia capabilities

Plan modeMission ControlSlash commands

Break big jobs into steps and check the result of each one.

Step 4 of 7

Build the pipelines

Maia Team builds orchestration pipelines that load data, transformation pipelines that cleanse and enrich it, and custom connectors for sources without a prebuilt one. It reuses shared pipelines and uses variables to keep work modular.

Your engineers see everything on the Designer canvas and can edit any of it.

Maia capabilities

Maia Team in DesignerCustom connectorsShared pipelines

Step 5 of 7

Validate as you go

Validate each pipeline against your warehouse to catch configuration errors before it runs. Sample the output of a component to see the data at that point, and check column metadata, without running the whole pipeline.

If something’s off, /fix identifies issues and suggests options, and /optimize suggests improvements.

Maia capabilities

Validate against warehouseSample data/fix and /optimize

Sample at each stage, not only at the end. Account admins can turn off Maia Team’s ability to sample data.

Step 6 of 7

Review and release

Commit and push changes to a branch, open a pull request, and merge once it’s reviewed. Pipeline review checks pipelines against your rules file.

Then promote through environments. Build and test in development, validate in staging, and run only stable, tested pipelines in production.

Maia capabilities

Pull requestsPipeline reviewEnvironments

Pipeline review needs a rules file that holds your standards. Set that up first.

Step 7 of 7

Run, monitor and improve

Schedules run pipelines on your cadence. Notifications tell you when a run finishes, or when its duration drifts from normal. Run history shows past runs, lineage traces data from source to destination, and Impact stream shows what a failed run affects downstream.

Use /document to generate documentation, and feed what you learn back into your standards so the next build starts better.

Maia capabilities

SchedulesNotificationsRun historyLineageImpact stream

Lineage is available on specific Maia plans.

Common questions

What teams ask before they start

Can Maia Team change production pipelines on its own?

Plan mode lets you review a plan before anything changes, and Mission Control tasks can work on a new branch. Changes then go through your normal Git review and environment promotion.

Who controls Maia Team in our account?

Account admins can turn Maia Team on or off, and can separately turn off its ability to sample data. Project and environment roles also control what people can do.

Do we have to use Maia Team to use Maia?

No. You can build pipelines yourself on the Designer canvas and use Maia Team where it helps.

Which data warehouses and Git providers does it work with?

Maia supports Snowflake, Databricks, Amazon Redshift and Google BigQuery. For Git, you can use GitHub, GitLab, Bitbucket, Azure DevOps or Maia Hosted Git. Component availability varies by warehouse.

Can we bring existing workloads?

Maia can convert workloads from a growing set of platforms, including dbt projects, SQL scripts, Informatica, SSIS and others. This feature is in public preview.

Are all of these capabilities on every plan?

Not all of them. For example, lineage is available on specific Maia plans. Check with your Matillion team for what your plan includes.
